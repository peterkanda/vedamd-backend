**To:** WHO Press — Permissions, World Health Organization, Geneva
**From:** [Name, title], VedaMD [legal entity, address]
**Date:** [date]
**Subject:** Non-commercial use of WHO guidance in a free clinical decision-support tool for health workers in Kenya and Sub-Saharan Africa

Dear Sir/Madam,

VedaMD is a free clinical decision-support tool for primary-care health
workers in Kenya and Sub-Saharan Africa. It works offline on a phone and is
also available to electronic medical record systems through an API. It
stores no patient data. Its clinical content is released under
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and is never sold.

We want to base more of our content on WHO guidance. Our understanding is
that post-2016 WHO publications are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO, and that
§4(b) of that licence lets us release adaptations under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0,
with WHO's citation and adaptation disclaimer. We respectfully ask for
the following.

1. **Confirmation of non-commercial use.** Health workers use VedaMD free of
   charge. Some work in private, fee-charging clinics, and some reach it
   through a commercial EMR system that their facility licenses. VedaMD
   charges nobody for the content. Please confirm that this counts as
   non-commercial use under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. If it does not, what
   permission would cover it?
2. **Titles published before the open licence** (all rights reserved).
   Permission to adapt the following into structured decision support, with
   attribution and a link to the original, distributed free:
   - *Pocket Book of Hospital Care for Children*, 2nd ed. (2013);
   - *IMCI chart booklet* (2014);
   - *WHO Model Formulary* (2008).
3. **SMART Guidelines licences.** The GitHub repositories give conflicting
   licences. `smart-anc` is CC0 1.0, but its source ANC Digital Adaptation
   Kit (IRIS 10665/339745) is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. `smart-immunizations`
   declares CC0-1.0 in its configuration but CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO in
   LICENSE.md. `smart-hiv` declares CC-BY-SA-3.0 in its configuration but
   CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO on its licence page. Which licence governs the
   clinical decision logic in each?
4. **ICD-11.** We embed ICD-11 codes and titles unmodified under
   CC BY-ND 3.0 IGO. We would like to add Kiswahili titles and local
   ICD-10↔ICD-11 mappings. What agreement does WHO need for this?
5. **Global Health Observatory data.** Does the CC BY 4.0 licence on
   data.who.int also cover indicator data retrieved through the GHO OData API?

We will cite each source as WHO specifies, add the adaptation disclaimer,
never use the WHO logo or suggest WHO endorsement, and replace adapted
content when WHO publishes a new edition. We would welcome WHO's comments on
our adaptations. Our content and its provenance are public.
