**Subject:** Request for a memorandum of understanding to use national clinical guidelines in a free clinical decision-support tool

Dear Sir/Madam,

VedaMD is a free clinical decision-support tool for health workers in Kenya.
It works offline on a phone and is also available to facility EMR systems
through an API. It stores no patient data. Every recommendation it shows
links to the guideline it comes from. Its clinical content is released free
under a non-commercial licence (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) and is never sold.

We want health workers to see the Ministry's own guidance first. We
therefore respectfully request:

1. **Permission to adapt national guidelines** into structured, cited
   decision support (dosing tables, referral criteria, red flags and
   step-by-step management), always attributed to the Ministry with a link
   to the source document. The priority documents are:
   - *Clinical Guidelines for Management and Referral of Common Conditions*,
     Volumes 1–3 (2024);
   - the Kenya ARV guidelines;
   - the national TB and malaria guidelines;
   - the NCD guidelines (hypertension, diabetes);
   - the Basic Paediatric Protocols;
   - the *Basic Obstetric Protocols* (2026).

   The Basic Obstetric Protocols already allow quotation and reproduction for
   non-commercial purposes. We ask to adapt them as well.
2. **Confirmation of the reuse terms of the Kenya Essential Medicines List
   (2023)**, which we understand may be "freely reviewed, quoted, reproduced,
   or translated … provided that the source is acknowledged".
3. **Structured data.** The KEML in a machine-readable form, and the terms of
   use of the Kenya Master Health Facility Registry API (for referral
   directories). Also a contact who can tell us when a guideline is
   revised, so superseded content can be withdrawn promptly.
4. **Clinical review.** VedaMD requires two clinical reviewers to sign off
   every record before it is published. We would welcome nominated
   reviewers from the relevant technical working groups, and advice on
   Kiswahili terminology.

We will follow any conditions the Ministry sets, including attribution
wording, version tracking and withdrawal of superseded guidance. We would
also share aggregate, anonymous information on which topics health workers
look up most often.
